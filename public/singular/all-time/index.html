The four views

Imputed lineup (the default) uses the full SiPM rating, box score plus lineup data, from 1996-97, the first season with lineup data. For earlier seasons it adds an estimated lineup part to the box-score rating: what lineup data show for later players with a similar box-score profile and a similar record of how their teams did with and without them. The estimate is cut back on purpose, so old stars are more likely understated than overstated. Figures that include it carry an asterisk; the 80% range under Best 5 and Career (and on hover) is how far the estimate could be off.

Box score, every era uses only the box-score rating, the one measure that exists from 1951-52 to today, so players from different decades are compared on the same footing. It cannot see most defense or the lineup effects the full rating captures: great defenders and big men are understated, and before 1973-74, when steals and blocks were first recorded, more so.

Best available uses the full SiPM rating from 1996-97 and, before, the box-score rating adjusted for how his teams did with and without him. That older measure spreads less at the top, so in this view recent players rank higher partly because of the measure.

Era-fair stretches or squeezes each season's ratings so the gap between the best players and the average matches the full rating's gap in 1996-2025; it assumes the true spread of talent is the same in every era.

Career, prime-based

Career starts from the prime: his minutes-weighted rating at ages 24-31. Three small adjustments sit on top, each a share of the prime. Load: minutes per season across the prime, measured against the star minutes of his era (from 10% off to 10% on). Early years (before 24) and late years (32 on): how he did against players with a similar prime and height at those ages. Ages before he was allowed into the league do not count. Seasons not yet played fill the prime at a cautious forecast (16th percentile); they can lower the early and late adjustments, never raise them. Playoffs count for a quarter of the prime: his playoff series at ages 24-31, each measured against the average player's playoff drop in that round, shrunk toward his regular-season prime by how many playoff minutes he has. Deep runs can lift him more than early ones; a poor late-round series never pulls him down harder than a poor first-round series would. † = an active player whose figure includes forecast seasons (hover for how many); player pages show the breakdown.

What each era can measure
  • 1951-52 to 1972-73. Box score without turnovers; steals, blocks and the offensive/defensive rebound split were not recorded and are estimated for each player (from his box-score profile and, for players who played on past 1973-74, his later recorded seasons), with 80% ranges; plus team results with and without the player; minutes often estimated (R² 0.58 against the full rating on held-out seasons).
  • 1973-74 to 1982-83. Box score with some stats filled from season totals, plus team results with and without the player (R² 0.70 against the full rating on held-out seasons).
  • 1983-84 to 1995-96. Full box score, plus team results with and without the player; no lineup data (R² 0.73 against the full rating on held-out seasons).
  • 1996-97 to 2006-07. Full sipm, fitted with the model's final weights (approximate).
  • 2007-08 on. Full sipm.
